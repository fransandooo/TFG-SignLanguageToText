\chapter{Objectives}
\setcounter{section}{0}

This chapter defines the purpose of the project, decomposes it into specific and verifiable objectives, and states how each objective is validated in the remainder of the thesis. The objectives describe the research and project outcomes pursued by the work. They are therefore different from the software requirements presented in Section~\ref{sec:requirements}, which specify the functional and non-functional behaviour expected from the implemented system.


\section{Main objective}

The main objective of this Final Degree Project is to design, implement, and experimentally validate a research prototype for word-level American Sign Language (ASL) recognition from short video clips, using hand-landmark representations and temporal neural models. The purpose is not to build a production-ready translator, but to study whether a compact geometric representation of hand motion can support feasible and reproducible isolated-word sign recognition.

The project also has an accessibility motivation. By exploring automatic recognition of ASL glosses, the work studies how computer vision and deep learning techniques can contribute to assistive communication technologies. The technical system is therefore a means to evaluate a recognition approach, its limitations, and its ethical implications, rather than an end product intended for direct deployment.

\section{Specific objectives}

The main objective is decomposed into six specific objectives. Each one is defined as a measurable project outcome and is later mapped to a validation method in Section~\ref{sec:objective_validation}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SO1: Analyse the technical and scientific background of sign language recognition.} This objective covers the relevant foundations of computer vision, pose estimation, MediaPipe Hands, sequence modelling, recurrent neural networks, GRUs, and attention mechanisms, with emphasis on their applicability to word-level sign language recognition.

    \item \textbf{SO2: Define a landmark-based data representation for ASL video clips.} This objective focuses on transforming raw ASL video clips into normalised sequences of three-dimensional hand landmarks suitable for temporal modelling, while documenting the datasets, preprocessing decisions, and limitations introduced by landmark extraction.

    \item \textbf{SO3: Design and compare candidate temporal models for word-level ASL recognition.} This objective evaluates alternative temporal architectures under a controlled WLASL30 benchmark and identifies the model most suitable for the available data regime.

    \item \textbf{SO4: Validate the selected recognition pipeline through reproducible experimental protocols.} This objective evaluates the selected model using standard classification metrics and protocols that reduce dependence on a single random seed or split, including multi-seed evaluation and stratified cross-validation.

    \item \textbf{SO5: Assess the generalisation and scalability of the recognition pipeline.} This objective tests whether the selected approach transfers to an independent ASL dataset and analyses how performance changes as the WLASL vocabulary size increases.

    \item \textbf{SO6: Contextualise the technical contribution through a runtime proof of concept and ethical analysis.} This objective integrates the recognition pipeline into an end-to-end mobile/server prototype as a proof of concept and analyses the accessibility, privacy, bias, reproducibility, and responsible-deployment implications of the approach.
\end{itemize}
